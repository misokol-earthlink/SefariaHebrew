Audio Attribution & Licensing

Temple Sinai, Sharon, MA: High Holiday trope audio selections are provided through the public High Holy Day readings made available by Temple Sinai of Sharon, Massachusetts. Audio playback is opened directly from the synagogue's website; the recordings are not copied, stored, modified, or redistributed by this application. Temple Sinai High Holy Day Readings.

Congregation Beth Shalom, Arlington, TX: High Holiday trope audio selections are sourced from the synagogue's publicly available High Holiday Torah cantillation recordings. Audio is played through a filtered in-application audio player using defined timing segments to omit spoken commentary and provide the Torah chanting portions of the recordings. Congregation Beth Shalom High Holiday Torah Cantillation.

Audio Selection Details

The standard Shabbat Parashot are established by Pocket Torah. The Parasha names are those used by Pocket Torah, as are the verses assigned to each aliyah and maftir.

Playback uses the public GitHub repository and its playback functions.

High Holiday Torah portions are not available for Pocket Torah playback using High Holiday trope. To provide such playback, recorded Torah portions are accessed from the public websites of Temple Sinai of Sharon, MA, and Congregation Beth Shalom of Arlington, TX. Links to these synagogue websites are available through the License Information link at the top of the main web page.

Each synagogue uses its own labels to identify Torah portions for the various holiday services, consistent with the labels and access links published on its website. Each synagogue has also divided its readings into aliyot and assigned verses to those aliyot.

This application preserves each synagogue's published reading names, aliyah designations, and verse assignments when identifying and accessing those recordings. As a result, the names and divisions used by one synagogue may not correspond directly to an apparently equivalent portion on the other synagogue's website. In addition, these divisions may differ from those expected under traditional aliyah assignment conventions. These differences reflect the organization of the source material and are intentionally preserved by this application.

Note that the references to Rosh Hashanah 1 and Rosh Hashanah 2 on the Temple Sinai site do not necessarily indicate the first and second days of Rosh Hashanah. They refer to the two readings published in the URJ Mishkan T'filah.
